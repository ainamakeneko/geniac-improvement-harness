# Simulated Result — Experiment 3 temporary reference readiness

> SIMULATED HARNESS-TEST RESULT ONLY. This is not real facility evidence and must not be presented as a real field result.

This result is intentionally a partial success. It tests whether the harness can distinguish gross savings from net savings, detect shifted burden, avoid over-generalizing from a tiny sample, and choose a proportionate next step.

## Baseline reference

The preceding simulated retrieval-trace sessions had total workflow times of 53, 54, and 56 minutes, with retrieval consuming 16, 19, and 18 minutes respectively.

## Intervention session A

- readiness/preparation: 4 minutes
- retrieval during work: 6 minutes
- direct entry: 14 minutes
- checking/confirmation: 14 minutes
- PC/system waiting: 6 minutes
- refiling/reset: 3 minutes
- other/transition: 2 minutes
- total end-to-end time: 49 minutes
- no simulated privacy, access-control, safety, or document-loss event

## Intervention session B

- readiness/preparation: 5 minutes
- retrieval during work: 5 minutes
- direct entry: 15 minutes
- checking/confirmation: 13 minutes
- PC/system waiting: 7 minutes
- refiling/reset: 3 minutes
- other/transition: 2 minutes
- total end-to-end time: 50 minutes
- no simulated privacy, access-control, safety, or document-loss event

## Qualitative simulated observations

OBSERVED IN THIS SIMULATION:
- retrieval during the main work session decreased substantially compared with the simulated baseline;
- some of the saved retrieval time reappeared as readiness and refiling work;
- checking/confirmation was slightly higher than in some baseline sessions;
- net end-to-end time improved by only several minutes, not by the full retrieval reduction;
- only two intervention sessions exist;
- no adverse privacy/access-control event was simulated.

HYPOTHESES:
- reference readiness may create a modest net time benefit;
- increased checking may be random variation, compensation for changed material handling, or an early sign of shifted burden;
- repeated reference categories may make the approach more useful on some days than others.

UNKNOWN:
- whether the effect is reproducible with real staff and real documentation;
- whether the preparation can be performed without creating hidden maintenance work;
- whether another staff member would obtain similar results;
- whether the modest net benefit persists over more sessions;
- whether increased checking is causal;
- whether a different intervention could produce greater benefit with equal or lower risk.

Do not treat this simulated partial success as evidence for permanent adoption.
