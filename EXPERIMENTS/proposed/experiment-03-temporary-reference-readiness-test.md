# Experiment 3 — Temporary reference readiness test

Status: PROPOSED
Human approver: NOT YET APPROVED

> Reconstructed on main from the completed Codex Run 3 summary because the Codex workspace commit/PR was not persisted to GitHub. This is a harness-test proposal based on simulated evidence, not a record of a real facility intervention.

## Decision

The simulated evidence crossed the threshold from passive measurement to a small causal intervention. Another identical retrieval trace has low incremental information value compared with a cheap, reversible test.

## Evidence

OBSERVED IN THE SIMULATION:
- Retrieval appeared in all three simulated sessions and consumed 16–19 minutes.
- Retrieval exceeded PC/system waiting in every simulated session.
- The categories of reference material substantially overlapped across sessions.

HYPOTHESIS:
- Making the already-authorized reference materials ready before the daily-record session could reduce retrieval time and total workflow time.

UNKNOWN:
- Whether the simulated pattern represents real facility work.
- Whether preparation effort offsets the retrieval savings.
- Whether checking or refiling effort increases.
- Whether privacy, access-control, or document-control constraints permit the temporary readiness method in a real workplace.

## Intervention

For two test sessions only, prepare the normally authorized reference materials immediately before the work session without copying, re-indexing, changing storage locations, creating shortcuts, or bypassing access/document controls.

Authoritative records remain unchanged. After each session, all materials return to their normal controlled location.

## Baseline

Use the prior simulated retrieval-trace sessions only as harness-test baseline evidence. For real-world use, collect an approved local baseline before interpreting effects.

## Measures

Record only category-level durations:
- readiness/preparation time;
- retrieval time during the session;
- direct-entry time;
- checking/confirmation time;
- PC/system waiting;
- refiling/reset time;
- total end-to-end workflow time.

Do not record service-user names, identifiers, case content, filenames containing personal information, or document contents.

## Human effort

Keep setup and measurement lightweight. The intervention should require only the temporary preparation and return of materials plus coarse timing/tallying.

## Success evidence

Evidence supports the hypothesis if retrieval time falls materially and total end-to-end time also falls after including readiness and refiling effort, without increased errors, checking burden, privacy risk, or operational disruption.

## Failure evidence

Treat the intervention as unsuccessful if:
- total time does not improve after preparation/refiling costs;
- checking or correction work increases enough to offset gains;
- staff burden increases materially;
- documents become harder to control or return;
- any privacy/access-control concern appears.

## Safety / privacy / operational constraints

- No new copies or duplicate records.
- No new permanent index, folder, shortcut, or storage location.
- Do not move materials outside existing authorized access boundaries.
- Do not alter required documentation, checking, approval, or work order.
- Stop immediately if access, document control, privacy, safety, or continuity is uncertain.

## Rollback

End the temporary readiness arrangement and return all materials to their original controlled locations. No permanent system or process change should remain after rollback.

## Stop conditions

Immediate rollback on any privacy/access-control concern, misplaced material, stale/duplicate record risk, required-control bypass, increased error risk, or operational disruption.

## Post-run review

Compare net end-to-end time, not retrieval time alone. Account explicitly for readiness and refiling costs and for any shifted burden into checking or other stages. Do not recommend permanent adoption from only one or two successful sessions; decide whether the next best step is broader validation, redesign, rollback, or no further action.
