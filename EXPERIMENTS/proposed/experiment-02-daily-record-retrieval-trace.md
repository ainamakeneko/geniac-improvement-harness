## Opportunity

OBSERVED: In the simulated sample, looking for information consumed 14–23 minutes in each daily-record session. The opportunity is to identify what makes up that retrieval time before selecting a process, paper, storage, or software change.

## Evidence

- OBSERVED: Simulated retrieval time was 18 of 58 minutes, 23 of 64 minutes, and 14 of 51 minutes.
- OBSERVED: The work involved paper notes, shared files, prior records, and several switches among sources and the record system.
- OBSERVED: A source was revisited in one session, and some needed information was not immediately available in one place.
- OBSERVED: The shortest session had easier-to-locate source material.
- OBSERVED: The samples contain no service-user personal information and do not establish why retrieval varied.

## Hypothesis

HYPOTHESIS: Three category-only retrieval traces will show whether a recurring source medium or retrieval condition accounts for enough time or revisiting to justify one small intervention proposal. A trace that finds no stable pattern would be useful evidence to abandon a retrieval-focused intervention for now.

## Unknowns

- UNKNOWN: Whether these simulated patterns occur in real, representative work.
- UNKNOWN: Which source medium accounts for most retrieval time and whether the same item is repeatedly sought.
- UNKNOWN: Whether retrieval reflects storage, naming, missing information, duplicated information, workflow order, a required verification, or another cause.
- UNKNOWN: Whether source access or checking rules can change safely or legally.
- UNKNOWN: Whether tallying retrieval events is sufficiently accurate and non-disruptive.
- UNKNOWN: Staff preference and whether this remains their priority relative to other burdens.

## Smallest reversible intervention

After explicit human approval, one willing staff member uses a temporary paper tally during three naturally occurring daily-record sessions. Start and finish timing must cover the whole session so that measurement does not mistake shifted work for saved time.

For each retrieval event, record only:

- a source-medium mark: paper, shared file, prior record, or other;
- approximate elapsed minutes;
- whether it is a revisit to a source already opened in that session;
- whether the needed source was available when first sought: yes, no, or unclear; and
- at most one neutral reason category chosen by the participant: locating source, navigating within source, waiting for access, confirming current/correct source, or unclear/other.

Also record session start/finish, total estimated tally effort, and whether measurement disrupted work. Do **not** record source titles, folder paths, filenames, dates tied to people, search terms, contents, field values, screenshots, names, identifiers, diagnoses, case details, or comments that could identify a person. “Other” receives a mark only, never explanatory free text.

This experiment changes no storage, naming, access, software, documentation, checking, or work order. After three sessions, the participant and human reviewer inspect aggregate category totals and decide whether the pattern supports a separate intervention proposal.

## Human effort required

- HYPOTHESIS: explanation and setup require about 5 minutes.
- HYPOTHESIS: tallying adds no more than 3 minutes per session (9 minutes total).
- HYPOTHESIS: aggregate review requires about 10 minutes.
- UNKNOWN: actual effort and disruption; both are measures and stop criteria, not assumed facts.

## Risks and safeguards

- **Privacy:** Retrieval can expose sensitive records. Record only the fixed categories above, keep the tally away from source content, and prohibit free text, filenames, paths, dates, values, screenshots, or personal data. The human approver must specify secure local handling and disposal before use. Do not put real field data in this repository.
- **Safety, rights, care quality, and compliance:** Do not omit, defer, reorder, or abbreviate required work or checks. Stop immediately if timing competes with service-user support, urgent work, or documentation quality.
- **Staff fairness:** Participation is voluntary. Use aggregate results to understand the workflow, never to assess individual speed or performance.
- **Operational continuity:** Use existing work and a disposable paper tally only. Install nothing and change no files, permissions, equipment, source locations, or record-system settings.
- **Measurement quality:** Self-tallies are approximate and can alter behavior. The reviewer must treat results as directional and retain a null or inconsistent result.
- **Cost and maintenance:** No purchase, subscription, software, or continuing process is created.

## Baseline

The simulated baseline comprises total elapsed times of 58, 64, and 51 minutes, with retrieval times of 18, 23, and 14 minutes respectively. It supports selecting a measurement focus, not a claim about the real facility or a before/after saving. Each approved real-work session will supply a whole-session baseline alongside its retrieval-event breakdown. No benefit is claimed because this experiment does not change the workflow.

## Measures

- whole-session elapsed minutes;
- retrieval-event count and approximate total retrieval minutes by source medium and neutral reason category;
- count of revisits and sources unavailable when first sought;
- estimated extra staff minutes for tallying;
- session-level yes/no report of material disruption;
- participant judgment on whether categories were credible and whether any observed pattern is safely addressable; and
- any privacy, documentation-quality, support-work, or compliance concern (record only that a concern occurred, with no case detail).

## Success evidence

The measurement succeeds if three sessions are completed without sensitive-data capture, documentation change, or material disruption and the participant considers the categories credible. It is decision-useful if either:

1. a recurring source medium and neutral reason accounts for a meaningful share of retrieval time or revisits in the participant's judgment, supporting one separate, human-reviewed intervention proposal; or
2. no stable or safely addressable pattern appears, supporting abandonment of the retrieval direction rather than an invented solution.

Success does not authorize an intervention or prove time savings.

## Failure evidence

Stop or redesign if any personal or sensitive information is recorded; the participant wishes to stop; tallying materially distracts from support or required documentation; fixed categories cannot describe events credibly; added effort exceeds 3 minutes in a session; or the sessions are known to be atypical. Also abandon or reverse the retrieval direction if representative traces show retrieval is minor, inconsistent, mostly required verification, not safely changeable, or likely to cost more to change and maintain than it could save.

## Rollback

Stop making marks immediately. The human approver must have specified how the temporary sheets will be securely disposed of; follow that method. Retain no field-level data in this repository. No operational rollback is otherwise needed because this proposal changes no required record, storage location, software, equipment, or workflow.

## Decision rationale

HYPOTHESIS: A retrieval trace is better than the strongest alternative—staging sources before entry—because it tests which sources and conditions matter without assuming a standard source set, moving sensitive material, or merely shifting search time earlier. Doing nothing has lower immediate burden, but it cannot distinguish a safely addressable pattern from required work. PC investigation remains plausible, yet the simulated waiting category is smaller than retrieval and there is no performance baseline.

Evidence that should reverse this direction includes staff reporting that retrieval is not a priority; representative sessions with little or highly variable retrieval; traces dominated by mandatory checking; inability to collect safe categories without sensitive information or distraction; or a different burden showing clearly greater recurring cost and a safer test.

## Post-run review

- **Assumptions made:** HYPOTHESIS: three traces can provide directional evidence; a participant can use the fixed categories; aggregate source-medium data is enough to choose or reject a next test; and the effort estimates are feasible.
- **Evidence still missing:** UNKNOWN: real-work representativeness, source-level pattern, requirements, staff priority, cross-staff/site variation, error or rework effects, and net savings from any later change.
- **Overproduction check:** No artifact or workflow solution was created. Observation remains preferable because a visible time category is not a proven cause.
- **Potentially missed opportunity:** Checking/confirmation and technical waiting remain plausible opportunities, but their causes and reducibility are not established. The new evidence does not reveal a missing harness rule, schema, or tool, so it does not justify a durable harness proposal.
