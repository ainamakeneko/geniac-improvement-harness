## Opportunity

OBSERVED: Daily record entry can take around one hour per day, while the cause is not established. The opportunity is to learn where that elapsed time goes before proposing any change to the system or workflow.

## Evidence

- OBSERVED: Daily record entry can take around one hour per day.
- OBSERVED: Slow PCs or the workflow itself may contribute, but the cause is not established.
- OBSERVED: Staff are busy and have limited time and money for improvement work.
- OBSERVED: Current systems, process ownership, rules, and the main sources of error or rework are not supplied.

## Hypothesis

HYPOTHESIS: Three short, category-level work samples will show whether a consistent portion of elapsed time is associated with system waiting, navigation/search, required entry, duplicate entry, interruptions, or exception handling. If no category dominates, that result will prevent a premature intervention and support a different observation design.

## Unknowns

- UNKNOWN: Whether around one hour is typical or exceptional.
- UNKNOWN: Which portions are active work, system wait, interruptions, duplicate entry, navigation/search, or exceptions.
- UNKNOWN: Whether patterns differ by staff member, day, site, record volume, or required workflow.
- UNKNOWN: Which steps are mandatory and which could safely change.
- UNKNOWN: Whether self-timing will noticeably alter the work.
- UNKNOWN: Whether staff consider this the best burden to investigate first.

## Smallest reversible intervention

After explicit human field approval, ask one willing staff member to make a category-level work sample during three naturally occurring daily-record sessions that they regard as reasonably representative. Use a blank paper tally or an existing timer; do not build or install software.

For each session, record only:

- session start and finish time;
- approximate minutes in broad categories: required reading/entry, navigation or search, computer/system waiting, duplicate entry, exception handling, and work-related interruption;
- number of interruptions and unresolved exceptions;
- a yes/no response to “Did timing materially disrupt this session?”; and
- an optional, non-sensitive one-line comment about the categories or measurement method.

Do not record names, identifiers, diagnoses, record text, case details, screen images, field values, or any information about an individual service user. Do not change, skip, accelerate, or add to required record work. If separating categories during the session is distracting, note only start/finish and interruption marks, then stop the category tally.

At the end of three sessions, a human reviewer and the participating staff member compare the category totals. This proposal does not authorize a workflow change; any subsequent intervention requires a separate proposal and human approval.

## Human effort required

- HYPOTHESIS: explanation and setup will take about 5 minutes.
- HYPOTHESIS: each tally will add no more than 2 minutes, for no more than 6 minutes across three sessions.
- HYPOTHESIS: review will take about 10 minutes.
- UNKNOWN: actual added effort and disruption; measure both rather than treating these estimates as facts.

## Risks and safeguards

- **Privacy:** Timing daily records could expose sensitive material. Use paper positioned away from record content, collect categories only, and prohibit names, identifiers, values, screenshots, or free-text case details. The human approver must confirm the local handling and disposal method before use.
- **Safety, rights, and care quality:** Do not change required documentation or priorities. Stop timing immediately if it distracts from service-user support, required recording, or any urgent work.
- **Staff pressure:** Participation must be voluntary, and results must be used to understand the workflow rather than evaluate individual performance. Report the three sessions together, not as staff productivity data.
- **Operational continuity:** Observe naturally occurring work only. Do not introduce software, modify the computer, or delay entries.
- **Measurement quality:** Self-timing may distort time or produce rough categories. Capture perceived disruption and treat the result as directional evidence, not a productivity audit.
- **Maintenance and cost:** Use a temporary paper tally or existing timer. No continuing tool or subscription is created.

## Baseline

OBSERVED: The only supplied baseline is that daily record entry can take around one hour per day; its cause and representativeness are unknown. Each observed session's total elapsed time is therefore both a baseline sample and the denominator for its category breakdown. No before/after operational benefit is claimed in this measurement-only experiment.

## Measures

- total elapsed minutes per session;
- approximate active-work and system-wait minutes by broad category;
- interruption count and approximate interruption minutes;
- unresolved-exception count;
- estimated extra minutes required to perform the tally;
- whether the participant reports material disruption; and
- participant judgment after the review: whether the categories are credible and whether one next test is worth proposing.

## Success evidence

HYPOTHESIS: The observation is useful if all three sessions are completed without sensitive data capture or material disruption and either:

1. a recurring category is large enough in the participant's judgment to justify a focused follow-up proposal; or
2. the samples show that the assumed burden or categories are not consistent, thereby changing the decision toward broader sampling or another opportunity.

Continuation is not automatic. A human must review the evidence and approve any next proposal.

## Failure evidence

Stop or redesign the measurement if:

- any sensitive or identifying information is recorded;
- tallying disrupts required work or service-user support;
- the participant does not wish to continue;
- categories cannot be distinguished credibly;
- the added effort is judged disproportionate; or
- the sessions are known to be too atypical to inform a next decision.

A null result is recorded as useful negative evidence; it is not a reason to invent a software solution.

## Rollback

Stop tallying and securely dispose of the temporary sheet according to the human-approved local method. No computer, required record, approval process, or continuing routine is changed. Retain only a de-identified aggregate result if the human approver permits it; otherwise retain no field data.

## Decision rationale and evidence that would change it

HYPOTHESIS: Daily-record observation is preferable to the strongest alternatives because it combines the clearest supplied recurring time estimate with low-cost, reversible measurement and does not assume a cause. Submission aggregation is the closest alternative, but its frequency, duration, and error burden are UNKNOWN. Paper approvals may be substantial, but approval rules are UNKNOWN and an intervention could create compliance risk. Event work may be urgent but may also be intermittent. Support planning and indigo-dye maintenance have greater privacy, care-quality, process, or safety concerns.

The ranking should change if real-workplace evidence shows any of the following:

- OBSERVED in a human-approved measurement: daily-record sessions are usually much shorter than reported, highly atypical, or not considered burdensome by staff;
- OBSERVED in a human-approved measurement: submission aggregation, approval chasing, or event preparation consumes more recurring time or produces more rework, delay, or stress;
- OBSERVED through staff choice: staff are unwilling to time daily records but are willing to observe another low-risk workflow;
- OBSERVED in a human-led review: a different opportunity has a clear, safe cause and a cheaper measurable test; or
- OBSERVED in a safety/privacy review: daily-record timing cannot be performed without unacceptable distraction or exposure.

## Post-run review

- **Assumptions made:** HYPOTHESIS: three sessions can provide directional evidence; one willing participant can categorize time approximately; a paper tally or existing timer is locally available; and the proposed effort estimates are feasible. None is treated as an observed facility fact.
- **Missing evidence:** UNKNOWN: comparative monthly burden, error/rework and service-user impact, staff preference, typical session volume, system behavior, required fields, regulatory constraints, and cross-site variation.
- **Overproduction check:** No solution artifact was created. Because the cause of the daily-record burden is UNKNOWN, observation is preferable to software or workflow redesign.
- **Possibly missed opportunity:** UNKNOWN: manual exception handling may be a cross-cutting opportunity, but the supplied material does not identify a safe, bounded instance to test. No harness limitation or repeated failure provides evidence for a durable harness proposal.
